# M05 Bad-channel candidates

Module guide · Applicable implementation: Workbench 1.0.0rc2 · Document version: 0.2

This module generates amplitude, noise, correlation, bridging and outlier indicators within explicit comparable-channel groups. It reports candidates and criteria, without automatically treating channel differences as technical faults or applying interpolation or rereferencing.

[Related specification](/en/standards#55-m05-channel-quality-candidates) · [Prerequisite: M02 Data characterization and check plan](/en/standards/modules/M02)

## 1 Purpose and scope

The module screens channel quality in scalp EEG. Comparison groups require an explicit reference and basis for comparability. Different references, bipolar derivations or mixed measurement conditions must not be grouped without confirmation.

M05 depends only on M02 and calculates its own measures. It does not depend on M03 or M04 detections. Source bad-channel labels are excluded from detectors and may only be used separately for external comparison.

## 2 Inputs and prerequisites

Inputs are selected EEG voltages, native sampling rate, original channel indices, analysis window and per-channel `comparison_groups`. Unknown references are not inferred from channel names.

PREP non-spatial group thresholds require at least four usable channels; otherwise, available descriptive measures are still retained. The current bridge and LOF adapters require a confirmed common or linked-ear reference, one comparison group containing all selected EEG channels, and their respective duration and channel-count gates.

## 3 Processing and methods

### 3.1 PREP non-spatial bad-channel indicators — available

BrainElecQC independently implements PREP non-spatial components; the main flow does not call full PyPREP. It records non-finite samples and extremely small dispersion, high-pass filters a copy, then calculates robust amplitude, relative high-frequency noise, windowed correlation and dropout within groups.

Amplitude is described by `0.7413 × IQR`, standardized using the group median and corresponding within-group scale. The high-frequency measure is the MAD of the high-pass signal minus the low-pass signal, divided by the low-pass signal MAD, followed by robust within-group standardization.

Absolute Pearson correlation matrices are calculated in complete 1 s windows. After setting the diagonal to zero, the 98% Hazen quantile is taken. Although an output field contains `max_abs_correlation`, the measure is this high quantile, not a literal maximum. Low-correlation and dropout candidates use their respective fractions of assessed windows.

Zero group scale or insufficient valid channels do not produce fabricated infinite standardized scores. The flow excludes spatial prediction, RANSAC, interpolation and robust average referencing.

### 3.2 MNE electrode-bridge candidates — optional, available

The adapter calls `mne.preprocessing.compute_bridged_electrodes`. It calculates electrical distance as the variance of channel-difference waveforms in fixed segments, then uses the distribution to estimate bridge candidates.

Outputs are channel pairs and distance evidence. `lm_cutoff` is the upper search bound for a local distribution minimum, not the final bridging threshold. The current interface does not return the actual decision boundary; neither a threshold nor abnormal time intervals are invented. Similar waveforms alone do not establish an electrode short circuit.

### 3.3 MNE LOF channel-outlier candidates — optional, available

The adapter calls `mne.preprocessing.find_bad_channels_lof`, using Euclidean distance and local outlier factors on channel vectors across the entire selection. Absolute outlier factors at or above the configured threshold produce channel candidates.

No additional normalization, filtering, rereferencing or automatic neighbor-count reduction is applied. Results depend on reference, amplitude distribution and the channel set. They describe whole-selection channel outliers, without time localization.

### 3.4 PyPREP spatial prediction / RANSAC — planned

The catalog retains the source, but this method is not run. Integration requires reliable electrode coordinates, sufficient spatial sampling and separate validation. Running non-spatial PREP does not establish that spatial bad-channel prediction was completed.

## 4 Outputs and interpretation

The basic evidence file is `M05_bad_channels.json`; optional bridge and LOF results are retained separately.

| Output | Units or scope | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `channels`, `candidates` | Original channel identity, component values and reasons | Review each criterion; a detection is not automatically a channel-removal decision |
| Amplitude and dispersion | V or µV as specified | Interpret with reference and physiological amplitude |
| Standardized scores, correlation, noise ratios and window fractions | Dimensionless | Check comparison groups and the denominator of valid windows |
| `groups`, `thresholds_active` | Usable-channel count and group status | An inactive threshold does not mean the group passed |
| Bridge electrical distance and candidates | µV², channel pairs | Pair counts are neither bad-channel counts nor abnormal-interval counts |
| LOF factors and candidates | Dimensionless, whole-selection channels | Relative outlier evidence, without abnormal-time localization |

Low correlation or large amplitude can also reflect physiological, pathological or coverage differences. Reports should retain raw waveforms and context rather than only a “bad channel” conclusion.

## 5 Configuration

These are current implementation settings and screening conditions, not industry acceptance limits.

| Method | Key default or gate | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PREP copy filtering | 1 Hz high-pass; complete 1 s windows | Within-group comparison requires at least four usable channels |
| High-frequency component | At fs above 100 Hz, a 101-tap low-pass with a 45–50 Hz transition band | Unassessed if sampling rate is insufficient |
| Amplitude outlier | Absolute robust standardized score above 5 | Current within-group criterion |
| Relative high-frequency noise | Robust standardized score above 5 | One-sided criterion, without absolute value |
| Low correlation | Fraction of assessed windows with quantile correlation below 0.4 exceeds 0.01 | Requires strictly more than 1% |
| Dropout | Fraction of assessed windows with zero low-pass MAD exceeds 0.01 | Must not be conflated with physiological low amplitude |
| Flat channel | Standard deviation or MAD below `1e-15 V` | Numerical flatness criterion |
| Bridging | 0.5–30 Hz, fixed 2 s segments, `lm_cutoff=16 µV²`, `epoch_threshold=0.5` | 16 µV² is a search upper bound, not the final threshold |
| Bridge applicability | At least 8 channels, 10 s and 5 complete segments; Nyquist above the upper cutoff | Current adapter execution gates |
| LOF | 20 neighbors, threshold 1.5, Euclidean distance | Absolute outlier factor at least 1.5 |
| LOF applicability | At least 8 channels and 10 s, with channel count greater than neighbor count | The default 20 neighbors require at least 21 channels |

PREP copy filtering requires at least `max(304, ceil(3.3 × fs / high-pass frequency) + 1)` samples. Bridge and LOF also require the confirmed reference and single comparison group described in Section 2.

## 6 Validation and limitations

The PREP non-spatial implementation has measure and boundary checks. Bridge and LOF execution has been checked on real public recordings. This does not establish independently validated candidate accuracy.

An adverse result must be retained: on two complete ds002034 recordings, the PREP non-spatial flow returned zero candidates, while source files labeled four bad channels in total. The cause of this discrepancy is unconfirmed. Different short-window results do not resolve it. The source bad-channel annotation criteria still require verification.

Independent expert artifact labels spanning devices, reference schemes and pathological contexts are currently lacking. Candidates require review with channel positions, waveforms, reference and experimental state; they are not automatically used for exclusion or interpolation.
