# M06 Physiological artifact indicators

Module guide · Applicable implementation: Workbench 1.0.0rc2 · Document version: 0.2

This module uses auxiliary ECG/EOG signals to examine event-locked EEG responses, and checks high-frequency bursts and supporting EMG evidence. Physiological events, general high-frequency indicators and auxiliary-supported candidates are distinguished. A correlation or high-frequency energy alone is not automatically classified as an artifact.

[Related specification](/en/standards#56-m06-physiological-artifact-indicators) · [Prerequisite: M02](/en/standards/modules/M02) · [Integrity dependency: M03](/en/standards/modules/M03)

## 1 Purpose and scope

The module applies to scalp EEG and auxiliary signals with confirmed roles and a shared sampling clock. ECG, EOG and EMG requirements are checked separately. Missing auxiliary signals leave the corresponding evidence unassessed; they do not establish absence of an artifact.

M06 does not perform ICA cleaning, regression-based artifact removal or automatic segment deletion. High-frequency bursts without EMG support remain general indicators. Even EMG-supported candidates require review with raw waveforms and event context.

## 2 Inputs and prerequisites

M06 depends on M02 and M03. Inputs include selected EEG voltages, explicitly assigned ECG/EOG/EMG channels, native sampling rate, original time coordinates and M03 integrity candidates.

M03 intervals provide guards for high-frequency checks to reduce misleading effects from missing samples and constant-segment boundaries. Selected EEG and auxiliary channels must have the same native sampling rate. Cross-stream clock alignment and resampling are not performed. Auxiliary roles do not establish the acquisition reference scheme.

## 3 Processing and methods

### 3.1 ECG-locked evidence — available

MNE `find_ecg_events` is called directly to detect ECG events; BrainElecQC then summarizes corresponding EEG segments. The default ECG detection band is 5–35 Hz with an automatic QRS threshold. Event-rate heuristics used in the automatic search do not classify heart rate as normal or abnormal.

Complete EEG segments from −0.5 to +0.5 s around events retain the original calibrated EEG, without additional baseline correction, rereferencing or segment rejection. Outputs include event-locked mean, median, standard error and amplitude descriptions. Detection of heartbeats alone does not establish cardiac contamination of EEG.

### 3.2 EOG-locked evidence — available

MNE `find_eog_events` is called directly with a default 1–10 Hz detection band. If no threshold is explicitly supplied, it uses one quarter of the filtered EOG peak-to-peak amplitude. Evidence is then generated using the same EEG event-locking framework as ECG.

With enough events, split-half and odd-even average-waveform consistency describe repeatability. No universal correlation or amplitude acceptance threshold is applied. Missing EOG, insufficient bandwidth and no detected events are recorded separately.

### 3.3 High-frequency bursts and EMG support — available

This BrainElecQC combined method uses MNE filtering and Hilbert primitives; it does not directly call `annotate_muscle_zscore`. On a copy, it suppresses measurable mains frequencies and harmonics, extracts and standardizes each EEG channel's high-frequency envelope, smooths it, and jointly checks envelope strength, power increase, non-mains components, band coverage and concentration of abrupt changes in sliding windows.

M03 integrity candidates, local constants and selection edges define protected intervals. General high-frequency candidates require all joint conditions. If same-clock EMG also supports the envelope strength and correlation conditions, the candidate is `emg_supported_burst`; otherwise it is `high_frequency_burst`.

### 3.4 MNE cross-channel high-frequency indicators — optional, available

The adapter directly calls `mne.preprocessing.annotate_muscle_zscore`. The current band is fixed at 110–140 Hz. EEG Hilbert envelopes are standardized, aggregated across channels and smoothed to produce time candidates.

These are global time indicators across selected channels, without individual-channel localization. Known acquisition filtering that does not preserve the target band blocks the method. Unknown filter history retains a limited status; even successful execution remains exploratory. Mains harmonics such as 120 Hz can confound interpretation.

### 3.5 ICA components / ICLabel — planned

ICA decomposition, ICLabel classification and automatic component removal are not implemented. Integration requires checks of data rank, valid coordinates, reference, filtering and model-training applicability. A catalog entry does not imply that component-level artifact identification has been performed.

## 4 Outputs and interpretation

Basic evidence files are `M06_auxiliary_events.json` and `M06_muscle.json`. Actual MNE high-frequency extension results are retained separately.

| Output | Units or localization | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ECG/EOG events | Original seconds and sample coordinates, event count and events per minute | Physiological event detections, not artifact ground truth |
| Event-locked EEG waveforms | Mean, median and standard error in V | Read with event counts and complete-segment coverage |
| Locked amplitude and consistency | Peak-to-peak and DC-removed RMS in µV; dimensionless correlations | Describes repeatable responses; no universal contamination threshold |
| High-frequency candidates | Original channel and time window, power in µV², envelope standardized scores | Joint screening evidence, without automatic cause identification |
| EMG support | Envelope peak, correlation and auxiliary-reference status | Supported evidence must be distinguished from unavailable auxiliary signals |
| MNE high-frequency candidates | Cross-channel time intervals | No individual channel identity is invented |
| Module status | Completed, limited or unassessed reasons | Missing auxiliary evidence usually gives `partial`, not “artifact-free” |

## 5 Configuration

These are current implementation settings, not industry acceptance limits for muscle, ocular or cardiac contamination.

| Component | Current default or condition | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ECG / EOG filtering | 5–35 Hz / 1–10 Hz; 10 s FIR; at least 10 s input | MNE detectors; Nyquist must exceed 35.5 / 10.5 Hz respectively |
| EEG event locking | −0.5 to +0.5 s; complete segments only | No baseline correction, rereferencing or automatic rejection |
| Locked-response resource limits | At most 1000 events and 2 million epoch elements per channel | Excess events are sampled uniformly over time for summaries, not selected by quality |
| Repeatability description | At least 4 events | Required for split-half and odd-even consistency |
| Basic high-frequency band | 30 Hz to `min(100, 0.45 × fs)`; bandwidth at least 20 Hz | At least 10 s input; available band differs between datasets |
| Basic high-frequency windows | 1 s, 0.25 s step; envelope smoothing at 4 Hz | Candidates may overlap; counts are not independent-event counts |
| Joint high-frequency conditions | Envelope z above 4 for at least 0.15 s cumulatively; power ratio at least 3 | Duration need not be consecutive; all remaining conditions must also hold |
| Spectral and abrupt-change conditions | At least 5 effective frequency bins; non-mains fraction at least 0.2; high-frequency/total-band power at least 0.01; maximum difference-energy share no greater than 0.25 | Used jointly; no single value replaces the complete rule |
| Integrity guards | M03 and local constant intervals expanded by 0.5 s on both sides; 0.5 s selection-edge protection | Local constant differences no greater than `1e-15 V` for at least 0.1 s |
| EMG support | Envelope z peak at least 2 and correlation at least 0.5 | Checked in context around the window; not an independent diagnostic threshold |
| MNE high-frequency extension | 110–140 Hz, threshold 4, `min_length_good=0.1 s` | This duration merges short good gaps; it is not a minimum artifact length |
| MNE extension applicability | fs above 280 Hz and at least 10 s | Blocked if a known high-pass is at least 110 Hz or low-pass at most 140 Hz; limited when filter history is unknown |

The basic high-frequency power baseline comes from the current selection, so changing the window may change candidates. The MNE extension and basic method differ in bands, aggregation and output granularity; they must not be treated as one detector.

## 6 Validation and limitations

Checks cover auxiliary-signal reading, event coordinates, limited states for missing auxiliary signals and actual extension execution on public recordings. There is no independent expert artifact-validation set sufficient to support general accuracy claims.

Eye movements, heartbeats, muscle activity, pathological high-frequency activity and device interference can share waveform or spectral features. Event-locked consistency and EMG support are evidence, not causal diagnoses. Clinical acquisition/display-filter recommendations do not directly define preprocessing for every algorithm; high-frequency methods require separate bandwidth and filter-history checks.

## 8 Optional research extensions (1.0.0rc3.dev7)

The FieldTrip extension uses absolute sample differences and the maximum channel z value to identify review intervals, without filtering, padding, channel localization or rejection; require at least 10 s. ASR requires explicit confirmation of at least 30 s of calibration within the window. Call asr_calibrate/asr_process on the same high-pass-filtered copy; retain calibration hashes, rank, M/T/A/B and processing comparisons. Block unconfirmed calibration, insufficient rank and unsupported preset sampling rates. Whole-record automatic recalibration is disallowed. Reconstruction differences are not scores or artifact fractions.
