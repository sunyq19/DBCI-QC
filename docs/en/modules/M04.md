# M04 Spectrum and interference

Module guide · Applicable implementation: Workbench 1.0.0rc1 · Document version: 0.2

This module describes the distribution of signal power across frequency, detects measurable narrow-band mains indicators, and separately reports background spectra, spectral peaks and alpha-band power. Spectral shape requires interpretation with acquisition and physiological context; it is not a quality grade.

[Related specification](/en/standards#54-m04-spectrum-and-narrow-band-interference) · [Prerequisite: M02 Data characterization and check plan](/en/standards/modules/M02)

## 1 Purpose and scope

The module applies to calibrated scalp EEG selections. Each component has its own duration, sampling-rate, frequency-coverage and power-validity requirements. If one component cannot be calculated, other available descriptive measures are retained.

M04 does not automatically remove mains interference or apply universal filtering and cleaning. The presence or absence of alpha peaks, spectral goodness of fit or band power does not directly determine channel quality. A dedicated slow-drift classifier is not implemented.

## 2 Inputs and prerequisites

M04 depends on M02, not M03. Inputs are selected EEG voltages, native sampling rate, original time coordinates and explicit spectral configuration.

Interpretation also requires the actual reference scheme, acquisition-filter history and assessed coverage. If acquisition hardware removed a band, absence of a peak in that band does not establish absence of the original interference. Non-finite values, insufficient duration or incomplete band coverage may leave a component unassessed.

## 3 Processing and methods

### 3.1 Welch power spectrum — available

The module calls SciPy Welch directly. Defaults are 4 s Hann windows with 50% overlap, per-window mean removal, one-sided power spectral density and arithmetic averaging. The actual window length is `min(N, max(2, round(4 × fs)))` samples, and FFT length equals window length.

Frequency resolution is `fs / actual window length`. Short inputs reduce the window length, so interpretation must use the reported resolution. Complete-window counts and tail coverage are recorded; uncovered data are not treated as observations.

### 3.2 Mains frequencies and measurable harmonics — available

BrainElecQC implements a Thomson-type F test using DPSS multitapers; it does not call Chronux directly. Defaults are complete 4 s windows, 1 s steps and 2 Hz full bandwidth, giving a time-bandwidth product of 4, 7 tapers and degrees of freedom of 2 and 12.

The test scans within ±2 Hz of 50 Hz, 60 Hz and measurable harmonics. A center frequency is included only if its center plus 2 Hz is strictly below Nyquist. Each window is linearly detrended. Bonferroni correction uses all channels, complete windows and unique scanned frequency bins; corrected p values below 0.01 identify narrow-band candidates.

The estimated sinusoidal amplitude is `2 × |A|`, a peak amplitude rather than RMS. Constant windows or windows without valid residuals do not receive fabricated significance. The proportion of overlapping windows with detections is not the union proportion of affected time.

### 3.3 Background spectrum and peak model — available

The module calls FOOOF directly to describe log power as a fixed-form aperiodic background plus Gaussian peaks. A knee is not fitted. The default fit range is 2–40 Hz, with local results in 30 s parameter windows.

Each fit independently requires at least 10 s, at least four Welch periodograms, coverage through 40 Hz, strictly positive power in the fitting band, and a minimum allowed peak width no smaller than twice the actual frequency resolution. Whole-selection and local fits are assessed separately; a short tail may remain unassessed.

Background exponent, peak centers and fitting error are descriptive measures. A high goodness of fit describes model fit to the spectrum and does not establish an artifact-free waveform.

### 3.4 Alpha-band power — available

Welch PSD is integrated by the trapezoidal rule after interpolation at band boundaries. Default absolute alpha power is the 8–13 Hz integral. Relative alpha power divides this integral by the 2–40 Hz integral and multiplies by 100%.

This measure includes the aperiodic background; it is not alpha peak height above the FOOOF background. Background modeling and alpha power are suboutputs of the same registered `spectral_model` component, not additional independent algorithms.

## 4 Outputs and interpretation

The main evidence file is `M04_spectral.json`.

| Output | Units | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `psd` | V²/Hz; frequency in Hz | Read with window length, overlap, valid-window count and resolution |
| `line_noise` | Corrected p value, F statistic, frequency in Hz, peak amplitude in µV | Narrow-band candidates and unmeasured coverage; does not mean interference was removed |
| Background `offset`, `exponent` | log10(V²/Hz), dimensionless | Depends on reference, frequency range and physiological context; not a quality score |
| Peak center, bandwidth, height | Hz, Hz, log10 power | FOOOF bandwidth is 2σ; multiplying peak height by 10 converts it to dB |
| Fit R² and error | Dimensionless, log10 power error | Describes model fit; no universal acceptance limit |
| `alpha_power` | Absolute µV², relative % | Band energy including background, not peak amplitude or RMS |

`partial` means that some spectral or mains components could not be assessed; read the specific reasons. No significant mains candidates does not mean all frequencies were assessed. Absence of an alpha peak does not establish a channel abnormality.

## 5 Configuration

| Component | Key defaults | Source and meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Welch | Hann, 4 s, 50% overlap, mean removal | SciPy Welch settings in the BrainElecQC wrapper |
| Mains test | 4 s windows, 1 s step, 2 Hz full bandwidth | Current DPSS/F-test settings |
| Scan and criterion | 50/60 Hz and measurable harmonics, ±2 Hz, corrected p below 0.01 | Statistical screening settings, not industry channel grades |
| Spectral model | 2–40 Hz, fixed background, 1–8 Hz peak widths, at most 6 peaks | Current FOOOF wrapper settings |
| Peak selection | Minimum peak height 0 log10 power, peak threshold 2 standard deviations | Model settings, not EEG amplitude acceptance limits |
| Local fitting | 30 s parameter windows | Current implementation setting; each window is checked independently |
| Alpha power | 8–13 Hz; relative denominator 2–40 Hz | Explicit band configuration that may change with settings |

These values configure current measurements and screening. Changed settings must be retained. Candidate counts cannot be compared without considering frequency resolution, measurable coverage and changes in the number of multiple tests.

## 6 Validation and limitations

Checks cover spectral outputs from public recordings and selected known mains-injection, constant-signal and frequency-boundary cases. They support units, time-frequency coordinates and implementation behavior, not complete sensitivity and specificity on independent natural-noise data.

Clinical display-filter recommendations do not directly replace internal method filters. Display bands and high-frequency muscle checks, for example, need different bands; purposes and settings must be retained separately. Reference changes, filter history, short windows and physiological state affect spectral shape. No universal spectral quality judgment across devices or tasks is provided.
